UK fractional CTOs are typically priced two ways: a monthly retainer from
around £700 up to roughly £9,600, or a day rate of about £1,000. Both are
published figures from working providers, not averages someone made up. For a
business with 1–20 staff, the number that matters is usually near the bottom
of that range — most owners need a few hours a month of senior judgement, not
someone embedded in the team.

The spread is wide because "fractional CTO" covers two very different jobs:
answering questions and reviewing decisions at one end, and effectively
running engineering at the other. Most of these providers are pricing for
tech companies with an engineering team to lead — that's the buyer their
websites are written for. A 15-person services or retail business rarely
needs that second job at all.

## What actually moves the price?

- **Days per week.** This is the biggest lever by far — 941 Consulting's own
  tiers go from £699 to £9,599 a month purely on how many days you buy, from
  async-only up to two days a week.
- **Advise versus build.** A CTO who reviews architecture and vets suppliers
  costs less than one who is also writing code, managing a delivery team, or
  running your infrastructure day to day.
- **Regulated or compliance-heavy sectors.** Finance, healthcare and anyone
  handling sensitive data pays more for someone who already knows the
  regulatory ground, because the alternative is paying twice — once for the
  work, again to redo it correctly.
- **Minimum term.** Shorter minimums (fcto.uk's engagements, 941's Breakthrough
  tier) generally cost more per day than a longer retainer, the same trade-off
  as any contractor rate.
- **Who's asking.** Published rates are aimed at technology companies with an
  engineering team to manage, because that's who searches for this service
  most. A retail, professional-services or manufacturing business with no
  developers on staff is buying a smaller, cheaper job even when it uses the
  same job title.

## Do you need a CTO at all?

Before shopping the table above, it's worth asking whether the job is really
a CTO's. Most 1–20 staff businesses don't need someone leading an engineering
function — they need one senior person who can be trusted to make the
technology decisions nobody else in the business is qualified to make:
which software to buy, whether a supplier's quote is reasonable, whether the
business is exposed on security, and how to think about AI without buying a
tool because a salesperson was persuasive. That's a different, smaller job,
and it costs less because it's advisory-weighted rather than build-weighted.

## FAQ

**Is a fractional CTO the same as an outsourced IT director?**
Not quite. "Fractional CTO" is the US-imported term and usually implies some
build or engineering-leadership work. "Outsourced IT director" describes the
advisory-only version more accurately for a UK small business with no
in-house tech team. See the comparison guide above for the full breakdown.

**Is a virtual CTO cheaper than a fractional CTO?**
The terms overlap enough that price is set by scope, not label. A provider
calling itself a "virtual CTO" at two days a month and one calling itself
"fractional CTO" at the same commitment will land at a similar price — check
what's included, not what it's called.

**How many days a month is normal?**
For a business without an in-house engineering team, anywhere from a few
hours to two days a month covers most of what comes up: decisions, vendor
reviews, and periodic security or AI judgement calls. Buying more than that
is usually a sign the business needs a hire, not a fractional arrangement.

**Why do some providers hide their pricing?**
Most fractional CTO and consultancy sites quote after a discovery call, which
is standard for bespoke, scoped work — but it also means you can't compare
options before you've given someone your time. 941 Consulting and fcto.uk are
unusual in publishing figures up front, which is why they're the two named
here: a claim you can check today is worth more than one you can't.
